## Life~Work

**Life:** I've been enjoying my free time after [last month's](https://kyleio.com/p/learning-log-2608/) layoffs at Etsy. I spent it on side projects, catching up with friends, and mental health days. And the summer heatwave finally broke, which made my afternoon runs more fun.  

**Work:** I made time for freelance projects, but my focus was finding my next full-time role. Between Etsy's alumni network, recruiters, and friends, I've landed 9 interviews so far. I'm taking my time to find a team I'd enjoy working with at a company whose mission I believe in.

## Projects

- [My portfolio](https://www.kylewjohnston.com) – relaunched last month. This month I refined the design, added motion, and rebuilt key case studies as web-based presentations, so I can demo prototypes and other interactive elements during interviews.
- [notebin](https://github.com/kylejohnston/notebin) – continued adding features to the iOS version of my minimal note-capture app. Now it supports iOS 27, Siri, widgets, and lock screen controls. Reach out if you want to try it via TestFlight!
- Antigrav – an Obsidian plugin to support [append-and-review](https://karpathy.bearblog.dev/the-append-and-review-note/)-style note taking. It lets you set a command to instantly move a text selection to the top of a note. My next step is to release it as an official community plugin.
- [mage](https://github.com/kylejohnston/mage) – a CLI wizard that resizes images and converts them to WebP. OptImage had been my go-to tool for this, but it stopped working in macOS 27, so I made `mage` as a replacement.

## Digital gardening
### Bookshelf
- [It Lasts Forever and Then It's Over by Anne de Marcken](https://books.kyleio.com/book/it-lasts-forever-and-then-its-over) – a zombie's-eye view of a post-apocalyptic world. Strange premise, but the perspective made a zombie feel relatable.
- [The Making of the Atomic Bomb by Richard Rhodes](https://books.kyleio.com/book/making-of-the-atomic-bomb) – an in-depth history of the splitting of the atom and its consequences. I think there's a parallel to AI: once scientists grasped the power of the atom, they warned government officials. Officials ignored them at first (where AI is now).  

### Links
- [The Lightbulb Computer](https://lightbulbcomputer.com/) – a conceptual project around spatial and ambient computing. I liked the kitchen use case: talking and pointing to place timers and recipes on different surfaces looks handy.
- [Glance Chrome extension](https://www.jasoncashdollar.com/glance) – a Figma-style design inspector for any webpage. It's a local Chrome extension, so proceed with caution. I've used it daily with no issues.
